\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Relative to the research questions:

\textbf{RQ1.} A hybrid LSTM-plus-physics architecture can be built and operated as a unified lunar forecasting stack. Softplus residual blending and a locked hazard-weight vector produce stable outputs suitable for dashboard use. That is an architecture result, not a claim of uniform skill.

\textbf{RQ2.} The unified interface improves operational usability relative to browsing separate hazard tools. Qualitative scoring supports high coverage and usability with only moderate validation depth.

\textbf{RQ3.} Under the locked train-2 blend, accuracy gains are modest and hazard-specific. Solar RMSE improves in both 14-day windows. Radiation does not beat the replica in either window. Temperature, moonquakes, and dust remain physics ties. Micrometeorite scores are circular under zero blend weight. The prototype almost-everywhere win is discarded for accuracy claims.

Overall, LIPAS is best treated as consultative decision support alongside agency products. Further work should add better radiation baselines, independent micrometeorite truth, fuller ablation archives, and stronger validation against continuous surface measurements where available.
