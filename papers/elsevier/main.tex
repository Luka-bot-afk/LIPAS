\begin{abstract}
LIPAS (Lunar Impact and Predictive Analysis System) is a hybrid forecasting and decision-support platform that combines machine learning with physics-based models for six lunar hazards: temperature, radiation, dust, micrometeorites, solar activity, and moonquakes. Public NASA and NOAA products, OMNI telemetry, and lunar archives feed a temporal long short-term memory network constrained by physics baselines, with results shown in a web dashboard for selectable lunar coordinates.
This paper reports three evaluation layers. A client-side prototype study first suggested a mean-skill lead against weak replicas, but that early path used a different model stack and scoring setup and is not the locked result; early temperature already failed a Diviner-class check. Locked train-2 studies over two 14-day windows then show modest solar gains (Window~A RMSE 4.10 versus 4.66; Window~B 4.99 versus 5.99) while radiation does not beat the replica (0.119 versus 0.102 in night-dominant Window~A; 0.0188 versus 0.00266 in quiet dayside Window~B). Temperature, moonquakes, and dust remain physics ties; micrometeorite skill is circular under blend weight zero. Qualitative comparison shows LIPAS high on coverage and usability but only moderate on validation. Mission simulation and ablation add operational and sensitivity context. LIPAS is best treated as consultative support alongside agency products, with substantial further work required.
\end{abstract}
