\title{Building and evaluating a cross-hazard machine-learning approach to unified lunar forecasting}

\begin{abstract}
LIPAS (Lunar Impact and Predictive Analysis System) is a hybrid forecasting and decision-support platform that combines machine
learning with physics-based computational models to predict the Moon's space-weather conditions. These conditions include
temperature, radiation, dust activity, micrometeorite impacts, solar storms, and moonquakes. By unifying space-weather data
products from NASA and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) alongside OMNI2 data, the LIPAS
platform processes the data through a temporal Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network. Users can view the LIPAS
space-weather predictions via an interactive, web-based dashboard for any lunar location.
This study aims to address three research questions. First, it documents how the hybrid model LIPAS was developed and what
was learned throughout the process. Second, it studies how operational usability can be improved in real mission scenarios compared
with existing prediction systems. Third, this research investigates whether integrating machine learning and physics-based modeling
into LIPAS improves prediction accuracy compared with existing single-purpose NASA and NOAA lunar environmental models.
\end{abstract}
