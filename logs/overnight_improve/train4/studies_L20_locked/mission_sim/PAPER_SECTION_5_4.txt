5.4 Operational Mission Simulations
A controlled radiation-driven EVA simulation was executed at Apollo 11 using the locked second-train hybrid model and an EVA duration matching Apollo 17 EVA-3 (7 hours 37 minutes). Quiet baseline drivers from 2026-07-12 were advanced in 5-minute steps while a documented storm injection was applied: solar/CME precursor rise at T+40 minutes, geomagnetic coupling rise at T+55 minutes, and a GOES-like proton surge at T+70 minutes. Identical caution/no-go radiation thresholds were used for LIPAS and the NASA physics replica (caution at 0.095 mSv/h, no-go at 0.15 mSv/h), while the NASA GOES path alerted from the proton-flux product alone.
LIPAS issued its first caution at T+50 min (0h 50m) and first no-go at T+85 min (1h 25m). The NASA GOES proton product reached caution at T+80 min (1h 20m) and no-go at T+105 min (1h 45m). The combined NASA stack (physics radiation plus GOES) reached caution at T+55 min (0h 55m) and no-go at T+70 min (1h 10m). Relative to the proton surge at T+70, LIPAS caution occurred at -20 minutes (negative values mean earlier than the surge). The recommended return call under LIPAS was LIPAS NO-GO at T+85 min.
This scenario is intentionally radiation-focused because radiation is an operationally decisive EVA abort driver. It demonstrates warning timing and return-call behavior under a shared script, not overall multi-hazard superiority.
